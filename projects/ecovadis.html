Corporate Sustainability

Building Sustainability
Into the System

From reporting and ESG data to regulatory interpretation and operational programs, my work at Dematic has focused on building the systems that turn sustainability commitments into something measurable, repeatable, and actionable.

My work in corporate sustainability began with individual assignments—an EcoVadis assessment, a reporting request, a new piece of legislation to understand. Over time, however, I came to see these efforts less as separate projects and more as parts of the same challenge: how do you build a sustainability strategy that actually works across a global organization?

At Dematic, my work as a Sustainability Systems Specialist has centered on building and connecting those pieces. I have worked across sustainability reporting, ESG performance measurement, regulatory interpretation, climate risk, operational initiatives, and external assessments—translating information from across the organization into systems that can support decisions and action.

The result is less a single deliverable than an evolving sustainability infrastructure: one designed to measure performance, understand expectations, identify gaps, coordinate responses, and continuously improve.

01 / Measure

Turning ESG data into a decision-making system

Sustainability strategy begins with knowing what is actually happening across the organization. I built an executive Power BI sustainability dashboard that consolidated ESG data into approximately 30 material metrics spanning emissions, energy, environmental performance, and other sustainability indicators.

The goal was not simply visualization. It was to create a common performance language across regions—making trends easier to identify, comparisons easier to make, and areas for improvement easier to prioritize.

Because the underlying data and dashboard contain confidential company information, the live dashboard cannot be reproduced publicly. This case study instead presents the system architecture, design logic, and analytical approach behind the work without exposing proprietary data.

02 / Report

Creating a global sustainability narrative

I helped lead the development of Dematic’s GRI-aligned Annual Sustainability Report, coordinating data collection and review across more than 30 global teams.

The process brought together environmental metrics, regional initiatives, operational developments, and broader sustainability performance into a single reporting structure. Beyond producing a publication, the work required creating repeatable workflows for collecting information, validating claims, resolving inconsistencies, and determining which developments were material enough to communicate.

Reporting became, in this sense, more than an output. It became a mechanism for understanding the organization itself.

03 / Benchmark

Using EcoVadis as a framework for continuous improvement

I managed EcoVadis assessments across multiple regions, coordinating evidence and responses across functions and geographies. The work contributed to top-tier regional performance and year-over-year improvements across assessments.

Earlier in the process, I helped consolidate five European operating-unit assessments into a single regional assessment, creating a more consistent approach to evidence collection and sustainability reporting.

But the value of EcoVadis extended beyond the medal itself. I used the assessment as a diagnostic framework: identifying documentation gaps, tracing weaker scores back to policies or implementation, and translating external evaluation criteria into internal opportunities for improvement.

04 / Interpret

Translating regulation into organizational action

As sustainability regulation expanded, another part of my role became understanding what emerging requirements actually meant for the business.

I monitored and developed response strategies around legislation including California SB 253, SB 261, and SB 54, alongside emerging requirements in other U.S. and European jurisdictions. The work required moving beyond simply tracking whether a law had passed: I compared statutory language, implementation guidance, reporting expectations, timelines, organizational applicability, and evolving interpretations to understand what each development could mean operationally.

I also developed a recurring legislative newsletter to translate these developments for internal stakeholders—turning dense regulatory changes into concise explanations of what changed, why it mattered, and what the organization should watch next.

This work became one of the areas that most interested me: the space between environmental objectives written into regulation and the practical systems organizations must build to respond to them.

05 / Assess Risk

Connecting environmental change to business risk

I helped develop the company’s first TCFD-aligned climate disclosure framework, connecting climate-related risks with governance, strategy, risk management, metrics, and targets.

The project required thinking about environmental change differently—not only as something to measure through emissions inventories, but as a source of physical, regulatory, financial, and operational risk.

This work helped connect sustainability reporting with broader enterprise risk thinking, reinforcing a lesson that runs throughout my work: sustainability becomes most useful when it is integrated into existing systems for making business decisions.

06 / Act

Turning strategy into operational change

Not every sustainability problem ends in a report.

I also helped translate sustainability priorities into operational initiatives, including supporting a metal-recycling rollout across 14 U.S. sites that diverted more than 280,000 pounds of metal from waste streams.

Projects like this provided the other half of the sustainability system: using the information surfaced through measurement, reporting, benchmarking, and stakeholder engagement to identify opportunities for tangible operational improvement.

Taken individually, these projects produced very different outputs: a dashboard, a report, an assessment, a regulatory analysis, a climate disclosure, and an operational program.

I see them instead as components of one system.

  • Measure what is happening.
  • Report it consistently.
  • Benchmark performance.
  • Understand what is changing externally.
  • Translate those changes into risk and strategy.
  • Act on what the organization learns.

That is what corporate sustainability has come to mean to me—not a collection of reports or commitments, but the infrastructure that allows an organization to understand its environmental responsibilities and respond to them systematically.
